# Research: Computer Use Is a Stop-Gap (and I Sell Browsers)

> The article argues that computer-use agents (UI-driving) are a transitional stop-gap for legacy-software automation: brute-force frontier models can reverse-engineer protocols and rebuild apps more cheaply than per-step GUI driving, so the durable lock-in is data, not the interface — and the author discloses his Steel conflict of interest up front. Research broadly **confirms** the category is real and shipped (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft all live by mid-2026), that the author's COI is genuine (he is in Steel's *growth* function, not just "at" the company), that AI can reverse binaries (Google's Big Sleep found a real SQLite exploit), and that GUI-driving is genuinely expensive at volume. It **complicates** the thesis more than the draft admits: the durability counter-case is strong (UiPath grew 13% to $1.6B in FY2026; SAP formally prohibited third-party AI agents from autonomous API call sequences in April 2026; agent reliability lags capability with ~95% of pilots failing). It **corrects** three load-bearing points: Claude Computer Use is *token-metered*, not "per-click"; "the moat migrates from artifact to asset" is the author's own compression, not a known aphorism (Jerry Chen's "New Moats" is the closest root); and the author's Whoop/ESP32 hardware-reversing anecdotes are ahead of any public record — no documented case of an LLM autonomously reversing a BLE/serial/hardware protocol exists to cite.

## Counter-evidence & risks

### Vendors are closing APIs, not opening them — the durability case has fresh 2026 evidence
The article leans on "everything will just get an API." The strongest counter is SAP's revised API policy (announced April 2026): it explicitly *prohibits* "API use for interaction or integration with (semi-)autonomous or generative AI systems that plan, select, or execute sequences of API calls" except through SAP-endorsed pathways. A top-tier enterprise vendor is walling off the API route for agents — which supports the "GUIs persist" side the article is arguing *against*. The Register framed it as a lock-in concern. Best evidence: [Automation Today](https://automationtoday.net/featuredarticles/sap-api-policy-update-sparks-concern-over-ai-access-and-erp-integration/) (primary policy language); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/04/29/ai-clause-in-new-sap-api-policy-provokes-lock-in-concern/) (403 on direct fetch).

### The RPA incumbents are profitable, growing, and absorbing AI — not being displaced
UiPath posted FY2026 revenue of $1.611B (+13% YoY), ARR of $1.853B (+11%), sustained GAAP profit, and a $500M buyback. Both UiPath ("Agentic Automation") and Automation Anywhere ("Agentic Process Automation") have repositioned deterministic RPA as the governed orchestration layer *over* AI agents. A named customer (Wesco Distribution) explicitly expects "robotic process automation will orchestrate the agents." The "interface automation is a dying stop-gap" framing is not supported by incumbent financials. Best evidence: [UiPath IR Q4/FY2026](https://ir.uipath.com/news/detail/431/uipath-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026-financial-results); [UiPath Agentic AI Report](https://www.uipath.com/newsroom/agentic-ai-report-findings).

### Agent reliability lags capability — which keeps deterministic UI automation necessary
Fortune (Mar 2026) reports reliability lagging capability; IEEE Computer reports ~95% of AI-agent pilots fail; Stanford AI Index figures (via secondary coverage) show ~37% lab-to-real-world performance gaps, with 95% per-step accuracy compounding to ~60% end-to-end across multi-step flows. These are exactly the conditions under which deterministic GUI automation stays necessary for mission-critical work even as agents handle the flexible long tail. Best evidence: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/24/ai-agents-are-getting-more-capable-but-reliability-is-lagging-narayanan-kapoor/); [IEEE Computer](https://www.computer.org/publications/tech-news/trends/ai-agents-fail-production).

### The "models reverse protocols" claim is thin for hardware — your anecdotes are ahead of the public record
After targeted searching, no public documented case exists of an LLM autonomously reversing a BLE, serial, or hardware protocol. The famous ESP32 air-purifier teardown used esptool, Ghidra, Flipper Zero, and Wireshark with zero AI involvement; all Whoop BLE/API reversals were human. Reviewers familiar with the space may challenge the Whoop/ESP32 examples as evidence of an industry trend. Best evidence (negative finding): [jmswrnr.com ESP32 teardown](https://jmswrnr.com/blog/hacking-a-smart-home-device); [Project Zero Big Sleep](https://projectzero.google/2024/10/from-naptime-to-big-sleep.html) (the one verified AI-binary-reversal example).

### Regulation (HIPAA, SOX ITGC) structurally favors human-mediated / deterministic automation
SOX IT General Controls require documented evidence of control effectiveness and human sign-off; HIPAA requires accountability for decisions affecting PHI. UiPath's CEO explicitly invokes compliance as why AI autonomy must be balanced with oversight. The mechanism is real and widely echoed, though thinly sourced in any single primary regulator document — do not overstate it as settled consensus. Best evidence: [Pathlock ITGC/SOX](https://pathlock.com/learn/itgc-sox-the-basics-and-6-critical-best-practices/); [Blue Prism compliance guides](https://www.blueprism.com/guides/compliance-automation/).
